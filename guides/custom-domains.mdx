---
title: Custom Hostnames
description: Serve a Rigbox app on your own domains, with automatic TLS.
---

Every app you expose through Rigbox gets a subdomain at `*.rigbox.dev`. To serve it under your own brand, or under your customers' domains, add **custom hostnames** to it. Each hostname works alongside the `.rigbox.dev` subdomain, so every URL stays active at the same time.

Custom hostnames work on public, private and invite-only apps. On a private or invite-only app, visitors sign in with Rigbox first; see [Private and invite-only apps](#private-and-invite-only-apps).

## How many hostnames an app can have

| Plan | Hostnames per app |
|------|-------------------|
| Free | 1 |
| Starter | 1 |
| Developer | 10 |
| Pro | 100 |

They're included in your plan. For more than 100 on one app, email support@rigbox.dev.

An account can add 50 new hostnames per hour. Every Rigbox account shares one Let's Encrypt certificate quota, and the hourly cap keeps one large import from holding up everyone else's certificates.

## Add a hostname

```bash
rig app hostname add --app $APP --hostname shop.example.com
```

The `--app` value can be an app id or name. The command prints the DNS records to add with your DNS provider.

**A subdomain** such as `shop.example.com` needs one CNAME record, pointing at a target that belongs to your app alone:

```text
✓ Added shop.example.com to app web.
  Add this record with your DNS provider:
    CNAME shop.example.com
          3f9a0c1be27d4a5f8e6b1c2d3e4f5a6b.hosts.rigbox.dev
  Rigbox checks DNS about every minute. To see the status, run:
    rig app hostname ls --app web
```

**An apex** such as `example.com` can't hold a CNAME, so it needs an A record plus a TXT record that proves you own the domain:

```text
✓ Added example.com to app web.
  Add these records with your DNS provider:
    A     example.com
          51.255.194.99
    TXT   _rigbox-verify.example.com
          rbv_8d1e0f3a2b4c5d6e7f8091a2b3c4d5e6
  Rigbox checks DNS about every minute. To see the status, run:
    rig app hostname ls --app web
```

| Hostname | Record | Name | Value |
|----------|--------|------|-------|
| Subdomain | CNAME | `shop.example.com` | Your app's target, ending `.hosts.rigbox.dev` |
| Apex | A | `example.com` | `51.255.194.99` |
| Apex | TXT | `_rigbox-verify.example.com` | The hostname's token, starting `rbv_` |

Each app's CNAME target is never given to another app, and each hostname gets a new TXT token. A record left over from a deleted app never verifies a new one. To move a hostname between apps, add it to the new app and point its DNS there: it moves once DNS no longer names the old app.

<Warning>
If your DNS provider proxies traffic (Cloudflare's orange cloud), set the record to **DNS only**. A proxy hides the CNAME and intercepts TLS, so Rigbox can neither verify the hostname nor get its certificate.
</Warning>

## Check its status

Rigbox checks DNS about every minute after you add a hostname. Once the records are in place, it gets a TLS certificate from Let's Encrypt, usually within a minute.

```bash
rig app hostname ls --app $APP
```

```text
shop.example.com
  active: Serving https://shop.example.com.

example.com
  waiting for DNS: The A record is in place. Add the TXT record to prove you own
  example.com.
    A     example.com
          51.255.194.99
    TXT   _rigbox-verify.example.com
          rbv_8d1e0f3a2b4c5d6e7f8091a2b3c4d5e6

2 of 10 hostnames on this app.
```

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| waiting for DNS | The records aren't in place yet. The reason says which one is missing. |
| getting certificate | DNS is verified and the hostname is routed. Rigbox is getting its certificate. |
| active | The hostname serves your app over HTTPS. |
| error | Something needs your attention. The reason says what to change. |

The app's **Access** tab in the console shows the same list.

Once verified, a hostname stays routed. Keep its CNAME, or its A record, pointing at Rigbox: if DNS moves away, its status changes to error until you point it back. You can delete an apex's TXT record after verification.

To list hostnames in scripts, use `--output json`, or call [`GET /apps/{id}/hostnames`](/api-reference/apps/list-hostnames). [`POST /apps/{id}/hostnames`](/api-reference/apps/add-hostname) adds one from your own code, for example when your customers connect their domains to your SaaS.

## Declare hostnames in rig.yaml

List an app's hostnames in its `rig.yaml`, and every `rig deploy` adds the ones the app doesn't have yet and prints their DNS records:

```yaml
name: web
port: 3000
start: npm start
visibility: public
hostnames:
  - shop.example.com
  - example.com
```

A deploy never removes a hostname, so hostnames you add with `rig app hostname add` or the API stay when you redeploy. To remove one, use `rig app hostname rm`. The single `customDomain` key is replaced by `hostnames`.

## Remove a hostname

```bash
rig app hostname rm --app $APP --hostname shop.example.com
```

The command asks before removing; `--force` skips the question. The hostname stops serving at once. Your app keeps its other hostnames and its `*.rigbox.dev` subdomain. Delete the DNS records afterwards.

## Private and invite-only apps

A hostname on a private or invite-only (`privileged`) app asks visitors to sign in. A signed-out visitor goes to rigbox.dev to sign in, then comes straight back to the page they opened. The same people get in as on the app's `*.rigbox.dev` address: only you on a private app, and you plus the emails you invited on an invite-only one. Anyone else gets the same refusal as on the subdomain.

The first time a visitor signs in on a hostname, rigbox.dev asks them to confirm it, because the site will see their email address. It asks again after 30 days, or sooner if the hostname is verified again, for example after it moves to another app. Sign-in works only once the hostname is **Active**, and Rigbox checks the hostname's DNS again each time someone signs in. If the DNS no longer points to Rigbox, sign-in stops and the hostname shows *no longer points to Rigbox* until you fix it.

A sign-in lasts an hour, on that one hostname. It ends sooner when:

- you remove the hostname, make the app public, or delete the app
- the visitor signs out of Rigbox in the browser they signed in with, or that Rigbox session ends another way, such as a password reset
- the visitor's account is disabled or deleted

Only a page load starts a sign-in. A signed-out `fetch` or image request on the hostname gets a `401` instead, so an app that calls its own API should handle `401` by reloading the page.

Your app gets the visitor's identity in the `X-Rigbox-Email` and `X-Rigbox-UserID` headers, as on the subdomain. Rigbox removes its sign-in cookies before a request reaches your app and ignores any cookie your app sets whose name starts with `__Host-rigbox_`. It answers `/__rigbox/login/callback` on each hostname itself, so your app never sees that path.

You can change an app's visibility without removing its hostnames.

## Troubleshooting

The reason under each hostname says what to change. The ones you might see:

| Reason starts with | What to do |
|--------------------|------------|
| *points somewhere else* | The hostname resolves, but not to Rigbox. Set the record to the value shown and turn off any DNS proxy. Check it with `dig shop.example.com`. |
| *Replace … A record with the CNAME* | A subdomain needs the CNAME, not an A record. |
| *The A record is in place. Add the TXT record* | Add the TXT record. Check it with `dig TXT _rigbox-verify.example.com`. |
| *The TXT record is in place. Point … A record* | Set the apex's A record to `51.255.194.99`. |
| *A CAA record … doesn't allow Let's Encrypt* | Your domain limits which authorities may issue certificates. Add `0 issue "letsencrypt.org"` to its CAA records. |
| *… no longer points to Rigbox* | DNS changed after verification. Set the record back to the value shown. |
| *Let's Encrypt hasn't issued a certificate … after an hour* | Rigbox keeps retrying. Check for a CAA record or a proxy in front of the hostname. |
| *… still points to another Rigbox app* | Another app serves this hostname. Point its CNAME (or, for an apex, the TXT value) at this app as shown, and it moves here. |
| *… now points to another Rigbox app, which serves it* | The hostname moved to the app its DNS names. Remove it from this app. |
| *The app isn't public* | Make the app public with `rig app share --app $APP --public`. |

DNS changes can take a few minutes to reach every resolver. Rigbox keeps checking, so there's nothing to re-run.
